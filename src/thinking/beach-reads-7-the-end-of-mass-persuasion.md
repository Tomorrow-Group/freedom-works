Still with us? Still vaguely pretending you’re not thinking about Q4 planning while sitting poolside? You’re not alone. That’s exactly why *Beach Reads* exists. Part provocation, part escapism. Think Marketing week meets Black Mirror. Entertainment that will actually make you think, and help you return to your desk with a new perspective…

Here's 7/8... Sci-fi vignettes written as user log entries from fractured realities. Every user lives in their own digital dimension. This piece is a series of dispatches from inside those worlds - strange, contradictory, deeply personalised. Think Borges meets Brave New World with TikTok filters.

**User Log: Reality Cluster 238A**

Name: Li

Age: 29

Feed includes: Biohacker threads, home fermentation reels, jazz memes, trauma-informed astrology

Ad served: Kombucha brand offering “gut healing + jazz vibes”

Outcome: Engagement + purchase

Note: Entire brand tone tailored via language model trained on Li’s Reddit history

**User Log: Reality Cluster 07B-South**

Name: Diego

Age: 45

Interests: Football, flat-earth theory, crypto fatigue

Ad served: Carbon-neutral blockchain wallet endorsed by ex-footballers

Outcome: Click-through, partial KYC

Note: Ad copy rewritten hourly based on algorithmic trust triggers

**User Log: Cluster BRR.404**

Name: Unknown

Feed language: AAVE meme dialect + rural Catholic Facebook posts

Ad served: “Don’t let them take your butter” (DTC dairy box)

Outcome: Viral within 3 micro-networks

Note: Brand unaware it was theirs

The age of one big idea is over.

Persuasion has splintered.

We now market in shards. Each polished to reflect back a version of truth the viewer already believes in.

Brands don’t speak *to* people anymore.

They simulate them.

We used to fight for a 30-second ad slot.

Now we fight for coherence in a thousand simultaneous versions of the brand.

Mass reach?

Outdated.

Mass relevance?

Still possible.

But only if you’re willing to fragment, translate, mutate.

And trust that across all these micro-realities, the centre still holds.

Sort of.
